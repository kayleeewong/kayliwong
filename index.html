High school · Starting point

Learning to step forward

I joined the U.S. Naval Sea Cadet Corps as a recruit, the Navy-oriented counterpart to ROTC. Every summer and winter brought a new training challenge and a chance to advance.

RecruitTrainingNext rank

After completing recruit training, I could attend specialized medical and engineering programs. Experiencing both fields firsthand gave me an early look at two possible career paths and sparked my interest in where they intersect through biomedical engineering.

LeadershipMedicineEngineering

Beyond recruit training

02

10th grade summer · First laboratory

From equations to evidence

During a thin-film sputtering physics internship, I entered a real research lab for the first time. I learned to prepare samples, work with precise instrumentation, collect measurements, and interpret results that did not always behave as expected.

Published research Physical Review Applied · December 2025

Ferromagnetic resonance properties of multilayered (Fe80Ga20/B)n thin films grown on LiNbO3 substrates

I am a coauthor on this paper examining how multilayer design affects ferromagnetic resonance linewidths in magnetostrictive thin films, with implications for high-sensitivity magnetic field sensing.

Read the paper ↗
Math had always helped me make sense of complex problems. In the lab, I saw quantitative thinking become tangible and contribute to a published scientific result.
03

High school · Expanding the field

Science became bigger than one subject

As I joined biology and engineering clubs and followed vaccines, oncology, wearables, medical devices, and vision science, I realized that my curiosity was not confined to one discipline or technology.

OncologyVaccinesDigital healthMedtechVision science

Growing up near Seattle also meant being surrounded by people working in technology, engineering, and medicine. I began asking family friends and professionals what they built, which problems mattered, and how ideas reached patients. Those conversations helped me recognize that the questions energizing me were broader: not only how one technology worked, but how scientific progress, competition, adoption, and market needs shaped an entire industry.

04

College freshman year · Stanford

A quantitative path into biology

At college, I was still deeply curious about science, but I wanted to approach biological problems through a more quantitative lens. Biomedical Computation let me combine biology with statistics, computer science, and mathematical problem solving.

Biology+Statistics+Computer Science=Biomedical
Computation

I could use data to find patterns, build models, and ask better questions about complex biological systems. It felt like a continuation of everything I had explored before.

Studying at Stanford

College gave me the space to connect quantitative problem solving with the biological questions that first drew me toward healthcare.
